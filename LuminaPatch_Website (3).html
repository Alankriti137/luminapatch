The Problem

Medicine watches episodically.
Cancer grows continuously.

BRCA-positive patients and breast cancer survivors have no continuous monitoring between clinical appointments. All current surveillance is entirely episodic — a mammogram once a year, an MRI every 6 months at best.


Between those appointments, breast tissue is completely unwatched. If a tumour begins forming the day after a clear MRI, it will not be detected for up to 6 months. During that window it can progress from Stage 1 to Stage 3 — and the difference in survival is catastrophic.


A 2026 retrospective cohort study of 722 BRCA mutation carriers confirmed that even with intensive 6-monthly alternating MRI and mammography surveillance, interval cancers — tumours developing between scheduled scans — still occurred. The gap between appointments is a documented, measurable clinical vulnerability.


Source: Evaluating high-risk breast cancer surveillance outcomes in BRCA mutation carriers. Breast Cancer, Springer Nature, February 2026.

Status Quo

What exists today — and why it's not enough.

Every current breast cancer monitoring tool shares the same fundamental limitation: it produces a snapshot at a single moment in time, then leaves the patient completely unmonitored until the next appointment.

📷
Mammography
The standard screening tool for most women. Uses X-ray imaging to detect tumour masses. Produces one data point per year. Misses 15–20% of cancers in average density tissue and up to 50% in dense breast tissue. Accumulates radiation with every scan.
1 data point/year
🧲
MRI
More sensitive than mammography. Reserved for highest-risk patients — BRCA carriers and those with prior breast cancer. Costs $1,000–$3,000 per scan. Requires intravenous contrast injection. Produces 2 data points per year at best. Still leaves 6-month blind spots.
2 data points/year
🖐️
Self-Examination
Detects lumps large enough to feel — typically Stage 2 or later, when the tumour has already grown to a detectable size. Has no molecular or vascular sensitivity to early-stage changes. Highly unreliable and operator-dependent.

The Solution

A wearable patch that watches your tissue every single day.

LuminaPatch uses four completely independent sensing channels simultaneously. A personalised AI model learns each patient's individual tissue signature — then monitors continuously for multi-channel shifts consistent with early tumour development. All four channels must agree before any alert is generated.

💡
NIR Spectroscopy
Near-infrared light in the 700–900nm range penetrates breast tissue up to 8cm. The patch measures haemoglobin concentration and tissue scattering — detecting the early angiogenesis and stiffness changes that precede visible tumour formation by weeks. A 2025 study from Simon Fraser University and BC Cancer achieved 94.7% sensitivity and 84.2% specificity distinguishing healthy from malignant breast tissue.
Source: Pouriayevali et al., Sensors, MDPI, February 2025
⚡
Bioimpedance
A low-amplitude alternating current sweeps across multiple frequencies through the tissue. Cancerous cells disrupt normal tissue architecture in ways that shift the electrical impedance profile measurably and consistently. A 2018 IEEE-published device combined NIR and bioimpedance in a single wearable breast cancer detection unit — validating that BIS can differentiate healthy from cancerous breast tissue.
Source: IEEE Conference on Biomedical Engineering, 2018
🌡️
Thermal Sensing
Developing tumours have elevated metabolic activity and abnormal blood vessel networks — both of which cause local tissue temperature to run 0.5–2°C warmer than surrounding healthy tissue. A passive thermistor array with 0.1°C resolution detects asymmetric heat patterns continuously, requiring no power for the sensing element itself.
Source: Medical thermography research, multiple clinical studies
🧬
Syn Bio Layer
Engineered microorganisms in a biocompatible hydrogel detect cancer-associated volatile organic compounds secreted through the skin above early tumours. When VOC concentrations reach threshold levels, the organisms activate a reporter circuit producing a measurable electrochemical signal — adding a molecular biological detection channel that electronics alone cannot replicate.
Source: Based on tyrosinase wearable detection, ACS Nano, 2023
Personalised AI — not population averages.
Every existing tool compares a patient to a population average. LuminaPatch compares each patient to herself — her own biological baseline established over two weeks of continuous monitoring. A change that is normal for one patient and abnormal for another only becomes distinguishable when you compare someone to herself, not to a statistical average built from millions of different people with different tissue densities, hormonal profiles, and genetics.
How It Works

Four steps. No clinic visit required.

LuminaPatch is designed to fit into a patient's life with minimal friction. Prescribed by an oncologist or genetic counsellor and worn like a plaster under clothing.

01
Apply
The flexible patch is placed directly on the chest over the area of concern. No special adhesive, no medical procedure, no clinic visit. It rests against the skin through gentle flexible contact and connects to the patient's phone automatically via Bluetooth.
02
Learn
Over the first two weeks, the patch learns the patient's personal tissue baseline across their normal daily patterns — menstrual cycle phase, hydration, exercise, sleep, and temperature. This personalisation step is what makes the system clinically meaningful and distinguishes it from every existing tool.
03
Monitor
All four channels run continuously in the background. The patient goes about their life normally. The app logs data silently — the patient can check their personal data timeline at any time and add their own notes about symptoms, medications, or lifestyle events that provide context for the readings.
04
Alert
When all four channels shift together in a cancer-consistent pattern for 72+ consecutive hours, the patient receives a calm, informative notification. They review their data, add context if they want, and choose whether to share a structured clinical report with their doctor. Nothing happens automatically without patient consent.

Technical Depth

All components exist today.

LuminaPatch integrates four validated sensing modalities on a single flexible substrate. The technical gap between current capabilities and a fully functional device is under five years. Every component technology has been independently validated in peer-reviewed research.

Physical Construction
Flexible PDMS substrate with serpentine interconnects
The patch is built on polydimethylsiloxane — a biocompatible, skin-conforming material used widely in wearable medical devices. Serpentine wiring architecture allows the substrate to bend without breaking circuit connections. The UNC ANIRP patch confirmed NIR sensors do not require ultraclose contact to function accurately, enabling comfortable continuous wear without adhesives.
Thickness: ~0.5–1mm flexible substrate
No bio-adhesive required for skin contact
Biocompatible materials throughout
NIR System
700–900nm multi-wavelength LED array
Multiple LED wavelengths optimised for differential absorption of haemoglobin and lipids. Photodetectors read returning scattered light at defined source-detector separations of 2–4cm, optimised for breast tissue penetration depth. A 2025 MRI-guided NIR system validated 3D breast tissue reconstruction using flexible optoelectronic strips with 2,304 source-detector positions.
Wavelengths: 700–900nm multi-wavelength
Penetration depth: up to 8cm in breast tissue
Sensitivity: 94.7% (SFU & BC Cancer, 2025)
Bioimpedance System
Multi-frequency AC sweep, tetrapolar configuration
Tetrapolar electrode configuration minimises contact impedance artefacts. Frequency sweep from 1kHz to 1MHz extracts Cole-Cole parameters — a validated mathematical model of tissue electrical behaviour that changes characteristically with malignancy. The AD5933 impedance analyser chip is commercially available, low-power, and compact.
Current: <1mA — below sensation threshold
Frequency sweep: 1kHz to 1MHz
Chip: AD5933 or equivalent
AI Architecture
LSTM personalised baseline model on ESP32
Lightweight time-series anomaly detection running on-device. Compares today's readings to the patient's personal established baseline. Requires all four channels to simultaneously exceed their individual deviation thresholds for 72+ consecutive hours before generating an alert — minimising false positives without sacrificing sensitivity to genuine biological change.
Target: detect signal shifts ≥4 weeks before imaging
Target: false positive rate below 5%
Alert threshold: 72-hour sustained convergence
Power System
Piezoelectric harvesting from breathing
A thin-film piezoelectric generator converts mechanical energy from chest wall movement during breathing into electrical energy stored in a supercapacitor. Supplemented by a thin-film photovoltaic layer harvesting ambient light. Duty cycling — activating sensing circuits for a measurement window then returning to low-power sleep — extends operational life indefinitely under normal daily activity.
Primary: piezoelectric from chest wall movement
Secondary: thin-film photovoltaic
No charging or battery replacement required
Syn Bio Layer
Engineered microorganisms in biocompatible hydrogel
Chassis organism: E. coli Nissle 1917 or B. subtilis — both established as safe for skin-contact applications. Genetic circuit: VOC-responsive promoter linked to electrochemical reporter. Kill switch: engineered self-termination if environmental conditions exceed defined range. Hydrogel matrix: PEG or alginate-based, maintains organism viability and prevents environmental contamination.
Chassis: E. coli Nissle or B. subtilis
Containment: engineered kill switch
Matrix: PEG or alginate hydrogel

FAQ

Common questions.

Does the patch diagnose cancer?
No. LuminaPatch is a continuous monitoring device, not a diagnostic tool. It detects sustained changes in tissue signals that are consistent with early tumour development and alerts the patient to seek clinical evaluation sooner. All diagnosis happens through standard clinical pathways — imaging, biopsy, and pathology.
How does it prevent false alarms?
The patch requires all four independent sensing channels to shift simultaneously in a cancer-consistent pattern for at least 72 consecutive hours before generating an alert. Each channel measures a completely different physical property of the tissue. Normal biological variation — menstrual cycle changes, hydration, exercise — almost never changes all four channels simultaneously. This convergence requirement is the primary false positive control mechanism.
Is the electrical current safe?
Yes. The bioimpedance measurement uses a current of less than 1 milliampere — well below the threshold for any sensation and thousands of times below any harmful level. TENS devices used in physiotherapy clinics use the same principle. The NIR light is non-ionising and produces no radiation exposure. The patch is designed for continuous daily wear indefinitely.
Who owns the data?
The patient owns their data entirely. The AI logs all sensor readings locally on the device and in the patient's app. No data is ever sent to a doctor without the patient actively choosing to generate and share a report. Patients can add their own notes, review their full timeline, and delete their data at any time.
Does it work for all breast cancer types?
The patch detects changes in the biological properties of breast tissue that are common across most breast cancer types — haemoglobin changes from angiogenesis, tissue stiffness from fibrosis, and temperature elevation from metabolic activity. These changes occur broadly across invasive breast cancers. Specific performance by cancer subtype will be characterised during the clinical validation study.
What stage is LuminaPatch at?
LuminaPatch is at the early concept and validation design stage. The scientific foundations for each sensing modality have been validated independently in peer-reviewed research. The next step is a $1M observational study to confirm the multimodal signal exists in real patient data before building any integrated hardware.
